Maya Rivera

A storyteller who uses light and time to reveal what often goes unnoticed.

How it began

Maya's fascination with photography started at age nine, when she found her grandfather's old film camera in a cedar chest. The first time she developed a roll in the darkroom, the quiet ritual of making an image — the waiting, the small surprises — became a kind of magic. From family portraits to afternoons spent watching city light shift across alleyways, she learned to look for the decisive moment.

What she likes to photograph

Maya's work sits at the intersection of documentary and fine art. She gravitates toward intimate portraiture and urban landscapes: candid street scenes, quiet domestic rituals, and the small geometries of everyday objects. She favors natural light, strong compositions, and a limited palette — black, white and the occasional pop of red — that helps the subject's quiet personality come forward.

The future

Looking ahead, Maya is planning a series that explores neighborhood storefronts and the people who keep them open after dusk. She also hopes to publish a photobook and run small workshops to help others discover their voice. Above all, she aims to keep making pictures that invite a closer look.
